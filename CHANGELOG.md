# Changelog

## Unreleased

- `adb.open_ports` asks again the ports that gave no answer. Over a slow link, hundreds of connections at once can crowd it until most of them time out, and a timeout was dropped like a refusal, so the one port that mattered could be missed. A port that timed out, or failed with a local error such as too many open files, is asked again in a second pass, `retry_concurrency` (200) at once with `retry_timeout` each (by default twice the first pass's wait, never under 4 s). A refused port has answered and is never asked again. The second pass ends after `retry_budget` (90 s), and after `2 * retry_timeout` when no port has answered it yet, so a link that drops everything is not asked again port by port. `retry_budget=0` leaves the second pass out. `connect=` replaces `asyncio.open_connection`, for a test.
- `adb.open_ports` keeps its connections at once under the process's open-file limit, less `adb.FD_RESERVE` (64). At 800 at once under a limit of 256 (what a macOS launch agent starts with), most connects failed with too many open files before they reached the host, and an open port among them was lost without a word. `adb.fd_room()` gives how many fit.
- `adb.open_ports` waits for one port as long as the link needs. With no `timeout` it measures the round trip first (`adb.round_trip`, a connect to a port nothing listens on, a refusal counting as the answer) and waits `adb.scan_timeout(rtt)`, four round trips, never under 2 seconds and never over 15. A fixed 2 seconds missed the wireless-debugging port of a device reached over a link of 700 ms. `reach(..., scan_wait=)` and `claude-ops adb-reach --scan-wait SECONDS` give a fixed wait instead.
- `iterm.launches_held(table, registered, jobs)` also reads registration. A launch macOS held back runs again after SIGCONT but is never registered with LaunchServices, so nothing can script it, and `restart_app` still ends in failure after the resume. So the guard now refuses while an app launched in the last `LAUNCH_WINDOW_S` (6 hours) is missing from `lsappinfo list`, besides when the newest launch is stopped. It also refuses when lsappinfo does not answer, because lsappinfo asks LaunchServices, the part that holds launches back (`launches_held` is None in the result). An app counts as a launch when its executable sits in no framework and in no other app's bundle, and it is not the program of a launchd job of its own (an agent's app bundle never registers). `Processes.registered()` and `Processes.jobs()` read lsappinfo and launchctl, and `parse_registered` and `parse_jobs` read their text.
- `iterm.restart_app` refuses, with nothing stopped, while the Mac holds app launches back. When the app launched most recently is stopped (state T), an iTerm2 stopped now would open stopped too, and every window with it. `launches_held(table)` gives the reason, and the result carries `launches_held`. `restart_app` and `start_app` also resume the iTerm2 they opened when macOS left its launch stopped, and only that one (an iTerm2 whose start is after the open, never another stopped process). The result says so (`launch_resumed`, `resumed`, and the message). `Processes.table()` reads ps once as (pid, state, seconds since start, command line), and `parse_table` and `etime_seconds` read its text.
- The meter's collector waits up to `meter.COLLECT_WAIT_S` (8 s, under the exporter's own 10 s timeout, so it never saves a batch the exporter has given up on and sends again) for another writer, and when the store is still locked, while it connects too, it answers 503 with `Retry-After`, which an OTLP exporter retries. It waited 5 s, and on a busy machine a reading pass held the write lock longer, so the request ended in `database is locked`, a traceback, and that minute's figures lost. `meter.connect` takes `timeout`.
- `heavy`: a job the gate holds says a new waiting line only when a kind of reason comes or goes (memory, pressure, swap, disk). The gate's words carry figures that move on almost every poll, and a job held for hours printed a line each time one moved, thousands in one log. The line still gives the gate's words as they read at that moment. The turn's words (who holds the slot, how many jobs go first) are said whenever they change, as before. `wait_key(held, why)` gives what a line is about.
- `heavy`: swap over `HEAVY_MAX_SWAP_GB` holds a job only while the free disk is under `HEAVY_SWAP_DISK_FACTOR` (3) times the swap in use. It used to hold one while the disk was under `HEAVY_SWAP_DISK_PCT` (12) percent free, which on a large disk holds every job with more than 100 GB free, far more than the swap could fill. `HEAVY_SWAP_DISK_PCT` is gone. Under `HEAVY_MIN_DISK_PCT` (10) free still holds a job whatever the swap. `machine()` gives the data volume's free GB as a fifth value, and `HEAVY_FAKE_MACHINE` takes it as a fifth number. A reading without it holds nothing for swap. `reasons(m)` gives each reason with its kind (memory, pressure, swap or disk), and `short(m)` joins their words as before.
- `heavy`: the note a waiter gives once its gate has held it for `HEAVY_OWN_LIMIT_NOTE_MIN` names what holds it in the gate's words, and says whether that is the machine or a limit set in the job's own environment, with the setting to lower only in that case. It used to blame "this job's own limits" and the free memory whatever held it. `status` says "held by its gate".
- `heavy`: with `HEAVY_HOOK_LOG` set, every `HEAVY_HOOK` call that failed (an exit code, a timeout, a command that cannot start) is one line in that file, with the time, the event, the chat, the job, the pid and why. A hook that timed out was ended before it could write anything, and a job's own output is often kept nowhere, so the one line on the job's output was the only trace.

- `service_watch` decides, with no side effects, whether a service that stopped answering may be restarted. `decide(readings, now, setting, restarts)` answers `none`, `recheck`, `wait`, `restart`, `off`, `unknown` or `give_up`. It restarts only after two or more readings in a row that say the service does not answer, at least a minute apart (a busy service can miss one connect), never when the service is switched off or its setting could not be read, and it gives up after three restarts in ten minutes that were not followed by an answer. `setting(spec)` reads from launchd, without special rights, whether the service is enabled and loaded.
- `worktree.change_id` diffs with no context lines by default (`context=0`). A context line is the base's text, so with git's usual three, a rebase that did not edit the change still gave a new id whenever the base had changed a line within three lines of it, as two branches that each append an entry to the same list do. Line numbers were already ignored. With no context, a change that only moves an added line to another place in the same file keeps its id. `change_id(..., context=3)` gives the old reading.
- `plain-voice` judges a `gh api --input` that is a JSON object by the values GitHub shows only (`title`, `body`, a release's `name` and a repository's `description`). The raw file was judged before, so `json.dumps`' own `"title": ` separator read as a colon in prose after every key and refused a call whose prose was fine. Input that does not parse as a JSON object is judged as text, as before. `plain_voice.shown(text)` gives the texts that are judged. `public-voice` and `plain-voice` also read `gh api --input -` from the command's here-document, which neither hook read before.
- The hooks' command reader took `);`, `)&&`, `)|` and a `)` before a newline as one word, so the command after a `$(...)` or a subshell was joined to the one before it and never judged. `L=$(cd repo-live && pwd); git -C "$L" checkout x` and `(cd /tmp); git -C repo-live reset --hard` went through `protected-checkouts`, and a line such as `X=$(date); F=/elsewhere/n.md; echo y >> "$F"` was refused because `F` was never read as assigned. Each part is its own token now. `$PWD` is the folder a `cd` earlier on the line moved to, not the hook's own folder. `protected-checkouts` no longer joins a write target that starts with a variable nobody on the line assigned (`$T/notes.md` after `T=$(mktemp -d)`) to the current folder, since its folder is unknown. It refuses one only when the rest of the path names a protected folder, and says to name the path in full. `commandline.write_targets` gives each write target as written, and `writes` keeps joining such a target to the current folder, the cautious reading for a hook that keeps text out of a folder.
- `worktree.change_id(root, head, base)` gives the id of `head`'s own change, `git patch-id --stable` of its diff from its merge base with `base`. A rebase that does not edit the change keeps the id, also for a branch stacked on another branch that was rebased under it, and any edit to the change gives another id. Keep the id when a change is read and compare it with the branch's new head later to tell a plain rebase from a new commit. It is None for an empty change or a commit that cannot be read.
- `worktree.last_checked_out(root, branches)` gives, for each branch, the folder whose checkout last moved to or from it and when, read from the HEAD log of the main checkout and of each linked worktree. A branch that no worktree has checked out now (its folder holds other work) can still be found in the folder it was worked on in, which `checked_out_at` cannot answer. A checkout from or to the branch and a rebase that ended on it count. A branch no log names is left out, and a removed worktree takes its log with it.
- `heavy`: a waiter that looked at the slot while its holder gave it back ended with `FileNotFoundError`, and its job never ran. `clear_stale` found the slot's folder with no holder record, then read the folder's age after the release had removed it. It now takes a folder gone by then as a slot given back. Twelve short jobs queued at once lost about one job in 45 this way before the fix and none in 360 after it.
- `service_check.Spec` takes `http_path`. A service with one answers only when a GET of that path returns a 2xx status within the timeout, because a port forward accepts a connection even when the program behind it is down, so a plain connect can read as up for a dead service. A proxy named in the environment is never used, and a reply that is not HTTP reads as not answering. An `http_path` without a port is refused.
- `rtk-rewrite` judges the rest of the first line after one leading `cd <folder> &&` and keeps the `cd` as it is, so `cd repo && git status` becomes `cd repo && rtk git status`. Commands that change folder first used to run as written and never got the short output. The folder must be one plain word (letters, digits and `_ . / @ + , : = % -`, with `~` at its start for the home folder). A quoted folder, one with a variable, `cd -`, `cd x; cmd`, `cd x || cmd` and a second `cd` run as written, and a plain grep's relative path is read against the `cd` folder.
- `update.held_paste(screen)` gives the size of a paste Claude Code holds unsent in its input line, in the lines its stand-in names (`[Pasted text #24 +20 lines]` gives 20, `[Pasted text #3]` gives 0), when the stand-in is all the line holds, and None for an empty line, typed words, a dialog or no screen. A program that pastes into a session and loses the Enter after it (for example while the session compacts) leaves its paste there, and `empty_prompt` then reads every later look as a half-typed message. The sender that made the paste can compare the size with its own text and press Enter once, which `inject.press(target, "Enter")` now sends (it sent only Escape). A stand-in can also be a paste a person is still writing, so nothing else should act on it.
- `update.last_activity` and `last_message` no longer count a resumed session's note of the background tasks the restart stopped (a task notification naming `__orphan_summary__`), or the dequeue of a queued message. A resume writes them as a queued message and a user record and starts no turn, so `idle` read a session that had just been resumed and sat at its empty prompt as one that "has written since its last turn ended, so it is working". A turn that does follow is seen through its own records, and a background task's own notification still counts.
- `rtk-rewrite` (PreToolUse hook, on Bash) puts `rtk` in front of the commands rtk has a short form for (git, gh reads, grep, cat, ls, cargo, pytest, pip, ruff, the JavaScript and Go tools and a few more), in the hook chain's own interpreter instead of a shell hook that starts jq and sed several times for each command. Only the first line is judged and changed. A grep or rg with any option, a gh write, a gh read with `--json`, `--jq` or `--template`, a `git commit` with a message file, a command that already runs rtk and a command with a here-document run as written. A plain grep on a folder gets `-r` when rtk is version 1 or later. It gives no permission of its own unless `--allow` is passed. `rtk_rewrite.rewrite(command, cwd)` gives the changed command, or None when it runs as written.
- `meter.attribute(session)` and `claude-ops meter items --session <id>` say what a chat spent on each work item over a window, from the item ids its turns name. A turn is the responses from one prompt to the next. A turn that names one item gives it every response (measured). One that names several gives each response to the last item named at or before it (split), and a prompt that pasted several shares the responses before its first call evenly (shared). A background job's notice goes to what the turn that started the job was doing (linked), and the rest is unattributed. A subagent's responses go where the call that started it was made. The items and the rest add up to the window's counts in `meter.report`, to the token, and `meter.assignments` gives the answer for each response, and `meter.jobs` the work each call started to run on after it, with when its notice came back. What an id looks like and which tools' inputs carry one are the caller's, in `meter.Marks` or a JSON file (`CLAUDE_OPS_METER_MARKS`, or ~/.config/claude-ops/meter-marks.json), and the store keeps only the ids it matched and where, never the text around them. `skip` leaves windows of a conversation unmarked, and their responses unattributed. A changed configuration reads every transcript again, and a store from before this reads each one once more with its counts kept.
- The meter's transcript reader goes one line at a time and parses a tool's result only when it carries a usage figure. Before, it kept every new line of a file in memory, so a first read of a large transcript held all of it. It commits each file as it finishes, so a read that is stopped keeps what it read.
- `public-voice` leaves a path under a temporary folder out of the whole-command check wherever it stands, after a redirection (`2>/tmp/x`) or attached to a short option (`-F/tmp/x`) too, and `$TMPDIR` paths. The folders are /tmp, /private/tmp, /var/tmp and the system's temporary folder. Such a path is never published, and the text a command publishes from it (a message file, a body) is still checked in full. A `-c user.email=X` is left out too when X is the email `git config user.email` already gives in the command's folder, read when the hook runs, since every commit there records it anyway. Any other email holding a listed term is still refused.
- `plain-voice` joins a GitHub text whose paragraphs are broken across lines instead of refusing it. It answers with an `updatedInput` that sends the joined text and an `additionalContext` line that says so, and gives no permission of its own. A GitHub MCP call gets the joined text. In a gh command only the words that carry the text change (`--body` and `--notes` become `--body-file` and `--notes-file`, a body file or a here-document is pointed at the saved file, a `gh api` field becomes `-F key=@file`), so the rest runs as written and the caller's own file is never edited. A command it cannot change exactly is refused as before, naming the saved file. A listed phrase is looked for in the joined text too. `plain_voice.review(payload, cfg)` gives the refusal, the changed input and the note, and `check` still gives only the refusal.
- The hook chain passes a step's `updatedInput` to the steps after it, so a later check judges the changed call and a later rewrite builds on the earlier one. The chain answers with the last `updatedInput`, which holds every change.
- `sessions.build_index(..., max_age=)` and `sessions.live_folders(max_age=)` can reuse an answer given less than `max_age` seconds ago, for a caller that asks many times in a row (a server that builds a page per request listed every transcript folder again for each page). The default of 0 asks every time and `force` always does. Each caller of `build_index` gets its own copy of the entries.
- `claude-ops detach` records the process group it starts for the session that started it, and `wide-kill` lets a kill of that group through. A detached command leaves the session's process tree, so the hook could not tell the group was the session's own and refused `kill -TERM -<pid>`. `killguard.record_group` and `killguard.recorded_groups` keep the groups in the claude-ops state folder (`CLAUDE_OPS_STATE_DIR` changes it), one file per session. A recorded group counts only while its leader is still the process detach started, so a pid the system reuses later never inherits the allowance, and a group recorded by another session is not seen. A failure to record never stops the command.
- `iterm.TmuxNotFound`: `tmux_by_tty`, `tmux_client_ttys`, `doubled_clients` and `detach_client` (and so `chat_tabs`, `color_chat_tabs` and `bind_chat_keys`) raise it when tmux cannot be started, with the path tried and the PATH it was looked for on. They used to answer no clients, which a caller could not tell apart from a tmux that runs with none attached, and a background job whose PATH lacked tmux's folder read every terminal as not showing tmux. A tmux with no server still answers no clients, and one that does not answer in time is still unknown.
- `url_check.ok`'s timeout is now a total for the whole answer, every redirect and read included, and it follows at most five redirects (`redirects=`), each target held to `problem` like the first address. Before, the timeout applied to each wait, so a server sending one byte at a time, or a chain of redirects, could hold the caller far longer.
- `url_check.ok(url, timeout=10, context=None)` says whether an https address answers 200 now, in one GET with no cookies or credentials, following redirects only to https, and names the status or the error when it does not. `url_check.problem(url)` says why an address can never pass (not https, a user name or password in it, no host) before anything is sent.
- `go_public.fork_guard_survey(repo, branch=None)` says what the fork guard would find in a repository without cloning or changing it. It returns whether the fork guard workflow is there, which workflows run on a schedule or on workflow_dispatch, and the jobs the lint would flag (so the pull request that adds the check would fail). `go_public.workflow_texts` reads the workflow files through the API, and `go_public.public_repos(owner, forks=True)` lists the public, unarchived repositories that `refresh` sets the secrets on (without forks when `forks` is False). `add_files` and `add_fork_guard` now return the address of the pull request they merged.
- `file_patch.apply(path, find, replace, contains, backup_suffix=None, dry_run=False)`: put a small hand-made change back into a file that an update replaced. The recipe is the text to find (it must appear exactly once), its replacement, and the text that is in the file only while the change is. It answers `present`, `applied` (read back from the file), `not_found`, `ambiguous` or `failed` and changes nothing unless it can make the change and prove it. The write keeps the file's permission bits and goes through a rename, and the first backup copy is never overwritten.
- `fork-guard` now also flags a scheduled job that acts on the repository through a known third party action in its `uses:` (`actions/stale`, `dessant/lock-threads`, `peter-evans/create-issue-from-file`), not only through a `run` step or an actions/github-script step. `go_public.add_fork_guard` installs that workflow into a repository through a merged pull request, the way the private-terms check is installed, and `add_files` now takes the pull request's branch, title, body and commit message so both checks share it.
- `sign_in.SignIn`: a new Claude login for one account, made in a config folder of its own so the login every session uses is never touched. `SignIn.start(email)` runs `claude auth login --claudeai` with `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` and `CLAUDE_SECURESTORAGE_CONFIG_DIR` set to a new folder and a stand-in `open` first on its PATH, so no browser opens, `url()` gives the sign-in page for the caller to show where the person is signed in, and `wait()` returns the login and its `oauthAccount` block once the person allows it. `SignInFailed` and `WrongAccount` (the page was signed in to another account) keep nothing, and every way out deletes the side keychain item (`side_service(folder)`) and the folder.
- `service_check`: reads whether a service is up, from launchd and from a real connection. A `Spec` names a launchd label and domain and a TCP port or unix socket, and `check(spec)` gives a `Reading` whose state is `up` (the connection was accepted), `down` (it was asked and did not answer) or `unknown` (nothing could be asked). The state follows the answer and not the launchd label, so a socket-activated daemon with no process is still `up` and a running process that does not answer is `down`. Only a connection is made, so a caller that restarts services can act on `down` alone.
- `wide-kill` (PreToolUse hook, on Bash) refuses a command that signals many processes at once, with no approval path: `kill -1`, `kill 0`, `kill 1`, a negative pid unless a process of that group descends from the Claude Code process running the hook (a group the session started, written as a number), `killall`, `pkill -u`, more than a few targets, and a pattern kill (`pkill`, or `pgrep` or `ps | grep` feeding `kill` through `$(...)` or `xargs`) whose pattern matches a protected process or more than a few processes. `kill <pid>`, `kill -TERM $!` and `lsof -ti:<port> | xargs kill` are let through, `bash -c` and `eval` are read inside, and quoted text and here-documents are data. The refusal names the safe way. The protected process names, launchd labels and paths are in `~/.config/claude-ops/wide-kill.json` (or `--config`, or `CLAUDE_OPS_WIDE_KILL_CONFIG`), and `claude-ops hook wide-kill` runs it.
- `killguard`: the check a program makes before it signals processes. `killguard.check(targets, own_groups, max_targets)` gives the reason a list of pids is too wide (pid 1, 0 or -1, a negative pid the caller did not start, more than three targets), `require` raises `Refused`, and `kill` checks and then signals. From a shell script, `python3 -m claude_ops.killguard 123 456` exits 2 with the reason when the targets are refused.
- `fork-guard` finds a workflow that runs on a schedule or on workflow_dispatch and reads or writes the repository's issues, pull requests or comments in a job that has no `github.repository ==` guard. Such a job runs in anyone's fork too, where it acts on the wrong repository or fails because the fork turned issues off. The check reads the shell of a `run` step and the script of an actions/github-script step, clears a job guarded on the job or on the acting step, and exits 1 on a hit so a pull request that adds one fails. `claude-ops fork-guard .github/workflows`, and it needs the `yaml` extra.
- `sessions.build_index(root)` without a `cache` now uses `default_cache(root)`, so asking for the index of another folder never writes into the index of Claude Code's own folder.
- `kept-out` (PreToolUse hook, on Bash, Write, Edit, MultiEdit and NotebookEdit) refuses a write whose new text matches a rule's pattern in a file the rule covers, such as a path into a folder of throwaway scripts written into a memory note or a skill that every later session reads. It reads the content of a Write, the new text of an Edit, MultiEdit and NotebookEdit, and text a Bash command writes into the file. A rule's files are globs on the path and on its real path, its text is a regular expression whose first group `allow` may let through, and its message says what to write instead. The rules are in `~/.config/claude-ops/kept-out.json`, and `claude-ops hook kept-out` runs it.
- `sessions.default_cache(projects)`: the session index is kept in a file that follows the transcript folder. With `CLAUDE_OPS_PROJECTS` pointing at another folder (a test's fixtures, a copy), the index goes beside that folder, so it no longer replaces the index of Claude Code's own folder, whose next read had to read every transcript again (about 50 seconds for 15,000 of them). `CLAUDE_OPS_CACHE` still names the file when set.
- `claude_ops.github_watch`: ready-made sources for `watch.run_source` on a GitHub project you follow. `release_tags(repo)` gives the tags of its newest releases, `pull_request_fingerprint(repo, number)` gives one id that changes when somebody comments on or reviews a pull request, or when it is closed or merged, and `parse_fingerprint` reads that id back into its fields. They use GitHub's public API with no credentials, two requests for a pull request and one for the releases.
- `ssh.greet(host)` runs `ssh -T` with no command, which is how a git host (GitHub, GitLab, a Gitea server) says whether it takes the key. Such a host exits 1 even when it does, so the words in stderr decide, and a host that was not reached comes back as `UNREACHABLE` as from `run`. `ssh.identity_files(host)` lists the keys ssh would offer it, in its order, from `ssh -G`.
- `union` refuses a merge that holds one key twice in a dict display (`{...}`) when neither side did, in a `--set` file too. Two branches that each changed or added the same entry would both be kept, and Python keeps only the last, so one side's entry was lost with no error. `union.repeated_keys` says which keys.
- `guarded-labels` (PreToolUse hook, on Bash and the GitHub MCP tools) refuses a session's own addition of a label that only the sessions its rule names may add: `gh pr edit` or `gh issue edit` with `--add-label`, `gh pr create` or `gh issue create` with `--label`, a `gh api` write to an issue's labels, and a GitHub MCP call that sets `labels`. A program that adds the label from its own process goes through, and taking a label off is never refused. The rules are in `~/.config/claude-ops/guarded-labels.json`, and `claude-ops hook guarded-labels` runs it.
- `changelog.Change.committed`: each change carries its commit time (ISO 8601 with its offset). For the host's merge commit that is when the pull request was merged, so a deploy can say how long each change waited between its merge and the deploy. `parse` still reads a log without the time, and then `committed` is None.
- `heavy`: `HEAVY_HOOK` names a command told when a job is `asked` (it starts to wait), `admitted` (it takes the slot), `released` (it gives back a slot it held, or ends after its turn) and `withdrawn` (it stops waiting without the slot), with the chat, the job and the pid that holds or would hold the slot. A machine whose other work is queued somewhere else can then count a running heavy job against that work. The hook is only told. It runs for at most `HEAVY_HOOK_TIMEOUT` seconds (5), and a hook that fails or hangs never holds up a job. The first failure is said.
- `turns.serve_order` and `turns.assign`: which of a machine's waiting jobs go next, by class and then by when each took its place, with short jobs ahead of long ones that have not waited long, and which free place (slot, lock) each takes. A long job that has waited `jump_s` is not passed, and a place named `short` is kept for short jobs. The rule is kept apart from how places are held, so a lock folder, a slot file or a database row can ask the same one.
- `project_board.Board.graphql` sends a read again when GitHub's servers failed to answer it (HTTP 502, 503 or 504), with the same three tries and growing wait as a refused stream. A write is sent again only after a 503, which says no server took the request. After a 502 or a 504 a write may already have been made, and a second `createIssue` is a second issue, so that is still raised at once. One such answer used to end a whole board pass with `BoardError`.
- `project_board.Board.graphql` sends a request again when GitHub refused its HTTP/2 stream (`REFUSED_STREAM`), up to three tries with a short growing wait. A refused stream was not processed, so a write is sent again too. gh does not retry it once the request body is written, and one refusal used to end a whole pass with `BoardError`. Any other failure is still raised at once.
- `gcal.Calendar`: add an event to a Google calendar and read it back by its id, with the two files a sign-in leaves on disk and only the standard library. The id comes from the title and the times, so asking twice or a retried insert leaves one event, and a read-back that differs raises `NotConfirmed`. An event a person deleted stays deleted unless `add` is told `revive=True`, and `can_write()` says whether the token may add events. `last_like` and `last_like_in` find the newest earlier event matching some words, for a job that takes the calendar or the length from how the same thing went last time.
- `google_api.GoogleSession` and `GoogleError`: the sign-in and the retries that `gmail.Mailbox` had, now shared. `Mailbox` and `GmailError` keep their names and behaviour, `account` picks an entry from a token file that holds several, and `scopes()` asks Google what the token may do.

Only a short list of variables a command needs (`BASE`, each with its reason), the ones named at the call site, and the PATH the caller gives. `REDIRECTS` names the variables that redirect a command rather than inform it (`PYTHONPATH`, `GIT_DIR`, `LD_PRELOAD`, `TMUX` and others); asking to keep or set one raises ValueError
- `ssh.run`, `ssh.copy` and `ssh.peer_online`: one way to run a command on another machine and copy files there. A host that could not be reached always comes back as a CompletedProcess with `UNREACHABLE` (255) and the reason in stderr, never an exception; a password goes on stdin, never on the command line; `peer_online` asks Tailscale whether the host is online in its own view. Each takes `env`
- `union --set FILE` (`settle_tree(sets=...)`, `settle_set`): a ledger whose entry lines are an unordered set merges three ways, keeping every line either side added and dropping every line either side removed, wherever they sit, so an entry added beside one the other branch removed no longer stops a rebase; a Python ledger must still parse
- `owners.most_specific(subject, owners, kind="like"|"glob")`: the owner whose pattern matches a subject with the most literal characters, so an exact address beats a wide one; SQL LIKE patterns or shell globs, a tie going alphabetically
- `accounts.write_item` sends the login as quoted text instead of hex: `security -i` cuts a line longer than 4,032 characters, and a real Claude login in hex is longer, so it was saved cut. A login too long even as text raises ValueError and is never written on a command line
- `accounts`: read and write a keychain login item (the secret on stdin, never a command line), an account's usage and profile, renewing a saved login, the usage-limit stop in a transcript (`limit_hit`), and `choose`, which picks the account to switch to: one usable now, only when the one in use is spent
- `remote`: which Remote Control session a transcript belongs to (`remote_id`, its newest `bridgeSessionId`, as the claude.ai Code list names it) and the page script that renames that row and reads the title back (`rename_js`), since a resume keeps the session's old title and `/rename` does not change it. `update.continue_after_error`: the pure rule for telling a session stopped on a transient API error to continue, once per error, only while recent, at most a few times an hour
- `iterm`: find and tidy iTerm2's windows through AppleScript. `windows` (id, tab count, frame, title), `dead_windows` (frames iTerm2 closed but left on screen), `close_empty_window` (only a window that holds no session), `set_bounds` (read back, for a window yabai has lost), `window_of_session`, `session_of_tmux_pane`, `window_of_ttys` with `tmux_client_ttys` (a tmux chat's window by its client's terminal, never by title). Each window is read inside its own `try`, and a listing that could not be taken is None
- `config.login_digest()` fingerprints the account Claude Code is signed in to (the account and organization ids in `~/.claude.json`), and `snapshot()` includes it. A running chat keeps the login it started with, so a fingerprint that changed after a chat started means the chat needs a restart to use the new account
- `claude-ops stall <path>` (`watch.stall`, `watch.stall_check`) waits on a session's transcript, a folder of them, or any file or folder a job writes, and says whether the session finished its turn (exit 0), a done file was written (0), the path is gone (0), the session stopped on an API error such as a usage limit (1), nothing changed for `--minutes` (1, stalled, with what the last record was waiting on), or it was still changing after `--max-hours` (2). A folder finishes when every transcript in it ended its turn. The clock and the sleep are arguments, so a test does not wait. `update.last_message(transcript)` gives the newest record in which the conversation moved, read from the end past the bookkeeping Claude Code appends after a turn, and `update.last_activity` uses the same rule for which records count
- `claude-ops heavy` (`heavy.run`, `heavy.wait_turn`): one heavy job at a time on a shared machine, taken in turn across chats, gated on free memory, swap and disk, run under a memory cap on its process group, with `hold`/`release` for CI runners, `--max-hold`, `--notify` through `HEAVY_NOTIFY`, and each finished job's peak memory kept in `HEAVY_DIR/done`. `footprint.footprint_gb` reads a process's memory footprint (top's MEM) with proc_pid_rusage, and answers None off macOS
- `chat.claude_pid()`: the Claude Code process a hook or any child of a chat runs under, found by walking up the process tree to the first `claude` (with `chat.parent_of` reading one step through `ps`, and a `parent` stand-in for tests)
- `claude-ops layering <root> --package <pkg> --marker <import>` (`layering.candidates`) lists the modules of a project that reach none of the given markers through their imports, followed all the way down the project's own modules. In a private project built over public packages, a marker is an import that means "this uses the private side" (its database driver, the module that writes its records), so a module that reaches none works without it and is a candidate to move into a package. `reaches` gives the import chain that ties a module to a marker. It is meant for a test in the private project that keeps a reviewed list of the modules that stay, with their reasons
- Four repository tools, each a command and a module: `claude-ops union` (`union.settle_tree`) settles a rebase conflict where both sides only added lines and refuses every other, with JSON merged by key, `--ordered` files listed once and `--journal` files kept append-only; `claude-ops secret-shapes` (`secret_shapes.scan`) refuses commits that add a line of a shape a secret scanner flagged before, from a shapes file the project keeps, reading every commit as the scanner does; `claude-ops mutate` (`mutate.run_mutations`) writes generated or hand-written mutations into a copy of a worktree and lists the ones the test command lets pass; `claude-ops plan-apply` applies a written plan's Write and Edit steps exactly. Every function that runs git or a test command takes `env`
- `iterm` and `claude-ops tab-colors`: in every iTerm2 window that holds two or more chats (tabs showing a tmux session named `claude-*`), each tab gets a color no other tab in that window has. A color the user set is kept, a chat can take back the color it had before (`assign`'s `remembered`), and new colors come from `PALETTE`. Reading and writing go through iTerm2's Python API (`pip install 'claude-ops[iterm]'`, and the API switched on in iTerm2's settings)
- `update.is_waiter` also counts a report that sets a variable from a substitution and tests it (`L=$(ls -t x | head -1); [ -n "$L" ] && grep ...`), and `{ ... }` groups: `[`, `test`, `true` and `false` read, an assignment's value is checked as its substitution, and a `|` or `;` inside a `$( ... )` no longer splits the command around it
- `update.is_waiter` counts a report after the wait that only reads: a `for NAME in ...` loop with its `do`/`done`, an `if`/`then`/`else`/`fi`, and the `gh` subcommands that read (`GH_READS`: `pr view`, `pr checks`, `pr list`, `run view` and the like). A chat whose waiter reported a few pull requests' states once its log ended was never idle, so it was never restarted. Every `$( ... )` is now checked as a command of its own, and a backtick is refused: an `echo "$(...)"` used to pass whatever the substitution ran
- `update.stopped_on_api_error(transcript)`: the transient API error a conversation stopped on, when its newest message is Claude Code's `isApiErrorMessage` record whose text starts with "API Error:" ("Connection lost mid-response", a closed socket, the API server out of reach or limiting requests) and nothing was typed or queued after it. The session then waits at its prompt as if its turn had finished, so a supervisor can send it a short message to continue. Usage limits, a prompt that is too long, billing and an expired login are not transient (`NOT_TRANSIENT`), since asking again cannot help
- `claude-ops merge <number>` (`merge.merge_when_green`) merges a pull request only when every check on its current head has passed, waiting for checks still running. It reads the checks from gh's JSON, refuses a pull request with a failed check or with no checks at all (`--allow-no-checks` to merge one anyway), and merges with `--match-head-commit`, so a push between the check and the merge is refused by GitHub. Waiting and merging as two steps had let a pull request be merged with two test jobs red
- `update.idle` and `update.ready` look through background shells that only wait: a loop whose body is `sleep N` (`until <test>; do sleep 60; done`), followed by nothing or only commands that read and print, sitting in its `sleep` right now (`is_waiter`, `waiting_shells`, `command_lines`). Such a shell loses nothing when its chat restarts, because the next instance is told which background commands did not finish. A chat that kept a few of these running was never idle, so it was never restarted. `ready` reads the command lines itself; `idle` uses them when given
- `chat.diagnose` looks for a start-up question only in the last lines of the screen, and never while Claude Code's input box is there (`chat.input_box`). It matched the words anywhere, so a chat at its prompt whose conversation quoted "Yes, I trust this folder" read as stuck on the trust question, and `chat.at_prompt` never saw it ready
- The session index follows a conversation that moved to another folder: every record names the folder it ran in, and the newest one is the entry's `cwd`, on a full read and on an incremental one. It kept the folder of the first record, so a chat resumed elsewhere was listed under the folder it had left
- `sessions.build_index` writes the cache through a temp file of its own: with one shared name, two processes indexing at once renamed each other's file away and the second failed
- The test suite never reaches the tmux server of the person running it: a session-wide fixture unsets `$TMUX` and gives tmux a private `TMUX_TMPDIR` under /tmp. Inside a tmux pane, tmux follows `$TMUX` before `TMUX_TMPDIR`, so a test's `tmux kill-server` cleanup ended the real server and every session in it
- `inject.send_tmux` loads the text into the tmux buffer through stdin (`load-buffer -`). It passed the whole text as one `set-buffer` argument, and tmux refuses a command longer than about 16 KB with "command too long", so a long message never arrived and the error text was all the sender got
- `update.tools_held(transcript)`: the deferred tool definitions a conversation holds now, each tool as its newest load since the last compaction recorded it, with its description and argument names. A resumed chat keeps those until it is compacted, so a restart does not give it an argument a server gained in the meantime. Compare them with the server's tools before telling a chat its tools are current
- `update.restart_iterm` and `update.restart_tmux` (when it builds the resume line itself) pass the chat's current title as `name`, so a chat renamed after it started comes back under its new name. They passed the start flags' `-n`, Claude Code wrote it back as the chat's title, and the rename was undone. `update.title_of` and `update.remote_control_of` read the transcript from its end, so a long one is not read whole (two seconds for a 1.8 GB transcript, now a few milliseconds)
- The session index reads a transcript that grew only from where its last read stopped, and writes its cache only when something changed. The chats in use are the largest files, and reading a 100 MB transcript whole on each change took about a second. `sessions.rc_url` reads the same way, and `sessions.live_folders` asks `lsof` once for every process instead of once for each
- The iTerm2 listings (`inject.iterm_sessions`, `inject.send_iterm`, `update.screen_of`) skip a window iTerm2 keeps in its scripting model after it closed, whose tabs cannot be read. One such window made every listing fail, so no iTerm2 session was found at all; an error after the session was found is still raised
- `update.background_agent` also sees a workflow's agents, which write one folder deeper (`subagents/workflows/<run>/`), and counts a workflow as running between its phases while its journal is recent and it has no final state file. A chat running a workflow no longer reads as idle, so a restart does not end the run halfway
- `update.files_seen(transcript, since=None)`: each file the conversation wrote (Write, Edit, MultiEdit) or read whole, with the time of that result. Check it before telling a chat that a file it loads has changed, because a chat that changed the file itself already knows. Compare with the file's modification time to see whether someone else changed it afterwards
- `update.unfinished_background(transcript)`: the background commands a chat started that never got a task notification, with their command text, so a chat resumed after a restart can start again what it still needs. `update.last_start(transcript)` gives when the newest instance started, to pass as `since`.
- `inject` sends only into an empty input line: a half-typed message, a question on screen or a screen it cannot read answers `not-ready` (exit code 5) and sends nothing. `--force` still skips the checks
- `update.screen_of` reads a tmux pane with its styles and `update.plain_screen` drops the suggested next prompt Claude Code draws dim in an empty input line, so `empty_prompt` no longer reads a suggestion as text somebody typed. `chat.pane_text(styled=True)` keeps the codes, and `update.TYPED` names the typed-text answer
- `update.resume_line(..., name=...)`: the chat's current name replaces the `-n`/`--name` (and a named `--remote-control`) it started with, so a restart does not undo a rename.
- `update.model_of` reads the model and effort level a chat last ran on from its transcript, and every restart and `chat.resume_command` pass them on (`--model`, `--effort`), so a model or effort chosen inside the chat survives a restart. A chat started with `--settings` keeps the model its settings file names
- `update.restart_into_tmux` moves a chat from an iTerm2 tab into tmux shown in that same tab, and `update.restart_tmux` restarts a tmux chat inside its own pane. Neither opens a window
- `update.ready` puts every check on a chat in one place, adds `update.background_agent` (a background agent keeps a chat busy), and names a question waiting for the person instead of calling the chat "working"
- `update.remote_control_of` and `update.title_of` read a chat's Remote Control state and title from its transcript, so a restart keeps Remote Control
- `update.front_app(bundle_id=True)` gives the bundle id of the app in front
- `config.descendants(pid, procs)`, every process under one, so a window can be traced to the chat whose process made it
- `chat.in_tmux`, `chat.plain_look`, `chat.attach_line` and `chat.server_context`. A plain tmux session passes the chat's title to the terminal tab, so the tab no longer reads "cd (tmux)"
- `chat.claude_command` and `chat.resume_command` take `settings`, a settings file for that chat alone (`--settings <file>`), and a restart keeps the flag
